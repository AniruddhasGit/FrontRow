DELIVERABLES - what each document must carry
  03_concurrency_model  The two-system guarantee end to end: which lock is taken when, in which
                        order, what happens when each one fails, and WHY the database guard is
                        the one that cannot be waived. The single most important document here.
  04_deployment         The process topology - how many containers, what each one costs in RAM,
                        which port each listens on, which health endpoint the load balancer calls
                        and which the container health check calls, and the memory arithmetic.
                        Everything AWS is (reasoned, not observed).
  08_failure_modes      Redis down . Postgres down . SQS down . SES down or rejecting . S3 down
                        or the poster missing . the simulated payment provider slow . a
                        container SIGTERM'd holding locks . a deploy during a rush . the poll
                        loop failing silently. For each: what the customer sees, what the
                        operator sees, and whether data is at risk.
                        SES DOWN IS THE ONE TO WRITE FIRST: the receipt is the customer's only
                        proof of purchase and only way into the hall, so a failure AFTER capture
                        leaves a customer who has paid and cannot enter (OPEN-EMAILFAIL-01).
  02_decisions_owed     Every Art X row still open, and what it blocks.
  03_the_deliberate_gaps  What this architecture knowingly does not do, and why that was
                        acceptable. No TLS until OPEN-TLS-01 is ruled. One Redis node with no
                        failover. One EC2 instance, so a deploy is a downtime window. Polling
                        rather than push. A simulated payment provider.
